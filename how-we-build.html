How we build

Thought through, revised, iterated, researched.

Idea → Research → Design → Build → Deploy

Our design philosophy

Garmin itself has called it an enormous influx of submissions to Connect IQ, and a lot of what arrives is made in bulk: one quick prompt, one design, a new color, publish.

We went the other way. Every FaceGrabs face is a collaboration that was thought through, revised, iterated, and then researched. Each one exists to do a specific job, and we can tell you why it works the way it does.

Slow on purpose, so what ships is worth wearing.

  1. Idea

    Start with a real problem. Ideas come from wearers, from our own wrists, and from the suggestion page. If we cannot say what problem a face solves in one sentence, we do not build it.

  2. Research

    Check what the evidence says before drawing anything. Steady scores a rolling week because the activity guidelines and the habit studies grade the week, not the day. We read the studies, and we say where they stop. Read the research brief.

  3. Design

    Make it kind, clear and personal. One idea per screen, plain words, and a bar that fits your fitness, not a stranger's. We are all on the same journey, just at different stages.

  4. Build

    Make it work on real watches. It is tested in the Garmin simulator before release. Small, quick and light on battery.

  5. Deploy

    Ship it, listen, and keep improving. Faces go to the Garmin Connect IQ Store with plain release notes, updates are meant to keep your settings and progress, and your feedback shapes the next one.

An example: The Case for the Week

Most activity tracking gives a verdict every midnight. The research points the other way: the activity guidelines grade the week, a single missed day does no measurable damage to a habit, and how a streak is displayed changes whether people carry on after a break. Steady is built on those findings. It scores a rolling seven days, forgives the light day, and saves its zero for a full week of nothing.

The five-page brief shows the evidence, the arithmetic, and where the evidence stops.
